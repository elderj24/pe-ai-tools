Margin Leak Finder

Reads a year of invoice lines and shows where margin is leaking: discounts beyond policy, customers below target margin, inconsistent pricing on the same product, and cost increases that never reached the price. Then it builds a repricing list, sizes the EBITDA, and drafts the customer letters.

How it works and assumptions

Discount on a line = list price − invoice price. Discount up to the policy limit counts as within policy; anything deeper counts as beyond policy.

Gross margin = invoice revenue − units × unit cost. Freight, rebates and handling are not in the data, so true margins on small orders are likely lower than shown.

Repricing: for each customer below target, the increase needed is cost ÷ ((1 − target) × revenue) − 1, capped at the maximum increase. The EBITDA added assumes the customer keeps the stated share of volume: kept × (new revenue − cost) − (revenue − cost), on the last 12 months.

Cost not passed through: the year is split at its midpoint. A product is flagged when its average unit cost rose more than 3% while its average net price rose less than 1%. Margin lost = second-half units × (cost rise − price rise) per unit.

Same product, different prices: for the products with the most revenue, each customer's volume-weighted net price is shown as a share of list. A wide spread usually means discounts are set deal by deal rather than by policy.

AI: the readout and letters are written by Claude from a compact summary of the numbers, never from raw invoices, and only when you press the button. Where Claude isn't available, the page writes a plain summary itself and says so.
