PE Plugin Guide

You ask Claude, in plain English, for the work a private equity deal team does: screen a teaser, test the seller's numbers, price the deal, write the IC memo. The plugin keeps one running record for each deal, so every step builds on the last, and it tells you what it checked, what it could not check, and what it saved.

Ask in plain English15 skills1 record per dealNo firm's criteria built in
New here? Read "Get started" and "What do you want to do?" below. The "All skills" tab is reference for when you want the full list. The plugin is sector-neutral and ships with a blank firm profile: tell it your sectors, size range and return targets when it asks.

Get started

Your first deal in three messages

Open a Claude session with a working folder (the deal record is saved there) and send these. Swap in your own deal name and files.

  1. Start a record

    Creates the deal's record in your folder. You only do this once per deal.

    /pe-new-deal Project Cascade

  2. Send the first document

    Attach the teaser or CIM and ask in your own words. The plugin picks the right skill and saves what it found.

    Screen the attached CIM for Project Cascade. Is it worth our time, and what should we ask the banker?

  3. Ask where things stand

    Any time: the stage, open questions, anything out of date, and the suggested next step.

    /pe-state-status deal-project-cascade
You never need the technical commands. The two slash commands above are shortcuts. Everything else is a normal request, and the plugin runs its own tools. It will ask you when it needs something only you know, such as your firm's return targets or which entry multiple to use.

How it works

Three ideas behind every answer

One record per deal

Each skill reads the deal's record before it starts and saves its findings back. So you can work in a new chat tomorrow and the next skill still knows which seller numbers failed and what the normalized EBITDA is. When new evidence changes something, earlier work that relied on it is flagged out of date until it is refreshed or confirmed.

Seller numbers are claims

A CIM, banker deck or management model can support a number but never prove it. A number is proven only by a document of record (a ledger, a contract, an invoice file) or independent work, and disproven the same way. Every brief shows which is which, so you know what you are relying on.

The IC's decision is locked

When the IC decides, the plugin records the decision word for word, with who decided, on what authority and when, and freezes the case it approved. Later portfolio reviews compare actual results with that frozen case instead of writing over it. A pass is recorded the same way.

What the save messages mean

✓ committedSaved to the deal record. ◔ pending_commitThe analysis is done and shown to you, but nothing was saved (usually because there is no working folder). ✕ blockedA check failed and nothing was saved. The message says why and what is missing.
Ground rules

What the plugin will not do

  • Treat a seller, banker or management number as fact.
  • Make up your firm's criteria. You give return targets, leverage limits and size ranges once; until then, reports say no criteria were applied.
  • Let the upside case, unsigned add-ons or unproven synergies set the price.
  • Choose the entry multiple for you. It shows a low, middle and high value and asks; if you don't answer it uses the middle and says so.
  • Record a recommendation as a decision. Only the IC decides, and the record needs who, on what authority and when.
  • Fill in a missing fact itself. It asks, or records the gap as unknown.
  • Give legal or regulatory advice. Legal points in a brief are starting points for counsel.
  • Put regulated personal data in a chat. Patient, consumer or employee records go in only de-identified or under the agreement that covers them.
  • Say something was saved when it was not.
Questions

Common questions

Do I need to know the commands or the file names?

No. Ask in plain English and attach the files. The plugin chooses the skill, runs its calculators and saves to the record. The commands in the technical reference are there for people who want to check its work.

Do I have to use the deal record?

No. Every skill works in a plain chat and shows you what it would have saved, marked pending_commit. The record is what lets later skills build on earlier ones, so use it for any deal you expect to pursue.

Why is an earlier piece of work marked out of date?

Something it relied on changed: a seller claim was disproven, a figure was restated, a case weight moved. The reason is shown on the flag. Ask Claude to refresh it, or, if the change does not affect its conclusion, to confirm it is still valid and say why. Nothing old is edited in place.

How does it reach a recommendation?

From six tests: is the thesis proven, are the open risks small or fixable, do we believe the value-creation plan, is there a margin of safety in the price, does the range of outcomes clear our targets with a downside near 1.0x, and how does our price compare with the market. It lands on Pursue, Pursue at our price, Pass with triggers to come back, or Pass, and states a walk-away price. The memo recommends; the IC decides.

What if the IC hasn't told me everything about its decision?

Tell Claude what you know. It suggests the details your message implies (for example "the investment committee, by vote, today") for you to confirm. If something is still unknown, the decision is recorded as reported with the gap named, and the case is frozen once the missing details arrive.

Which skills produce Word or Excel files?

Underwriting can build an Excel LBO model for the standalone platform. Every other skill returns a written brief plus an HTML version with charts.

Is anything sent outside Claude?

The record stays in your working folder, and the HTML briefs load nothing from the web. Skills may search the web for current market or regulatory facts and cite them with dates. Load only material you are allowed to share with Claude.

How do I tell it about my firm?

Two things, both optional until a skill needs them. The firm profile holds your sectors, size range and current portfolio, for the fit and overlap checks; it ships blank, and anything blank is reported as unknown. Your return targets, leverage limits and entry-multiple range are given once, with where they came from, and every calculator reads them. The plugin never fills either in itself.

Does the contract skill give legal advice?

No. It is a business-side review for the deal team and its counsel. It redlines only agreements your side is a party to (an NDA, or a contract your firm or a portfolio company is negotiating), and only from the Word original; for a target's existing contracts it reports the terms, the consents needed and what to ask for. Its positions are common buyer-side starting points until you give it your firm's own.

What does this plugin not cover?

This is the core set. It has no skill for unit economics by site or product, management assessment, add-on integration, board preparation or exit readiness. Ask anyway: the stack guide names the nearest skill and what it can and cannot do, and records the rest as open questions for the deal team.

Technical reference (for checking the plugin's work)

Firm criteria. Set once, with where they came from: pe_state.py criteria set target_moic=2.5 target_irr=0.20 max_entry_leverage=5.5 max_entry_multiple=13 --basis "IC guidelines, Nov 2026". This writes pe-state/firm_criteria.json, which the record and every calculator read. criteria show and check report any drift.

Saving. Each skill drafts its update from the live record (draft), previews it (apply --dry-run), then saves (apply). Every save is sealed and versioned; check catches a file edited outside the tool, and verify checks a synced copy.

Out-of-date work. The tool flags outputs whose inputs changed. An output confirmed still valid (outputs_reviewed) no longer passes the flag on to work built on it. A stronger verdict, such as untested to corroborated, does not flag anything.

Exceptions and evidence notices. An exception is a rule a record breaks (resting on a disproven claim, a case outside your criteria); each is accepted one at a time with who accepted it and why. Notices that conclusions rest on evidence not yet validated are one summary line.

Decisions and the freeze. freeze --intake lists what the snapshot will store and what the record already knows, and writes nothing. A decision missing its authority or date can be recorded as reported (unknown_fields); the freeze waits for those fields. freeze records an approval, freeze --outcome pass a pass, close checks the conditions at closing, and init-portfolio starts the ownership record.

Calculators. Returns come from lbo_returns.py, weighted cases and the walk-away from scenario_ev.py, valuation from valuation_calc.py, the QoE bridge and NWC peg from qoe_bridge.py, covenant headroom and liquidity from covenant_liquidity.py. Every brief carries a numbers-audit line saying how many figures were traced to the record, a calculator or a source.

Under the hood

Where everything lives

Skills never talk to each other directly. They read and write the deal record through one state tool, which checks every save and keeps the record, its history and readable views in your working folder.
